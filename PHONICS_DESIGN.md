# Phonics — the open Sound Safari park

Chapter 05 retains two distinct random parent-enabled A–H letters and their four school words. It now consists of two searches in one open 3D space, with four park companions: the selected Sesame Street story guide and three original friendly helpers, Pip, Toby and Milo. The guide always differs from the player's character.

## Round 1: find by initial sound
The voice asks for something beginning with the target sound. A checked sound clip plays after the spoken question when available; otherwise a clearly labelled parent-model control is provided. Letter names or invented TTS phonemes are never substituted for an isolated sound.

Either of the two source words with the target sound is accepted. Once the parent confirms the child's oral response, that word is excluded from further sound searches. Each letter is requested twice, so all four source words are practised without imposing an order within the letter family. NPCs join the same search after an eight-second reaction window and move at about one tenth of the player's speed. They may point nearby, but never collect an answer, change progress, or take rewards from the child. “More thinking time” extends their wait by ten seconds.

## Round 2: find by spelling
A helper holds a large 3D card showing a word such as **Fish**, with the initial **F** coloured orange and the remaining letters dark. The card is attached to the NPC, faces the camera, and stays inside the game world. Camera focus offers a clear first look; moving returns to following the player. A small “Look at the word card” control can focus it again. The voice asks the child to find what is on the card without reading out the answer first.

Parents select two or three distinct card words, default three. Both letter families are represented. Only those selected words are searched again. The child still has optional word/phrase models after trying, followed by nearby parent approval.

## World and movement
- Hen, goose, ant, bear, cat, dog and elephant wander slowly within reachable park areas.
- Fish swim inside a fixed pond. Fish and fin refer to the same animal; fin recognition uses its actual 3D fin rather than a duplicate fish display.
- Animals pause when the child comes close, while she speaks, and while a parent panel is open.
- Hats sit on benches, the pretend axe on a table, and the ball, nest/egg and small gate on the ground. Cut/dig retain their animated craft-table and ground-patch representations.
- NPCs wander before joining a search; the card holder walks slowly to a central spot. No NPC blocks movement or owns a collectible answer.
- The old token hunt, sound gates and delivery sequence are replaced by these two rounds. No learning window is opened.

## Parent controls and records
Letter pool approval, reroll, replay pair, optional parent-checked local sound clips, manual speaking approval, categorized notes, XP, three stickers and individual vocabulary saves remain. Records include the four sound-round words, the two/three card words, guide, letter pool, sound model mode, retries and parent observations. No microphone is requested, and no automatic pronunciation score is assigned.

Checks cover all 28 letter pairs with both card counts, word coverage, slow NPCs and grace timing, moving targets and pond limits, coloured card text, fin ray hits, speaking gates, guide replacement while a card is held, single rewards and saved notes. The animation-loop test follows 196 routes across all 28 pairs. Visual and audio behaviour on an iPad still require device verification when browser preview is unavailable.
