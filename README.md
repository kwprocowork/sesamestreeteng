# Sesame Street English Adventures — family prototype

Buildless static app: serve `dist/` over HTTPS. Bundled Three.js and local artwork; no external CDN or API keys. The welcome trailer is a 40-second cinematic montage of 20 solo park scenes, followed by a choice of three chapters. Exactly four unique friends are chosen from 19 reference-guided models on arrival and when “New friends” is pressed. Each of the 19 characters has five authored animation clips; each selected character appears once in each of the five themed chapters. Character-specific examples include Cookie Monster enjoying a cookie, Ji-Young strumming a guitar, Gonger cooking, Abby making magic, Tango chasing a ball, and Oscar playing peekaboo. Individual scenes change park decorations, background colour and camera angle, with a gentle dolly and short shaded dissolves. Only one character is visible at a time. Names and captions stay outside the canvas. The cast includes Elmo, Cookie Monster, Ernie, Bert, Count von Count, Snuffleupagus, Zoe, Julia, Gonger, Rudy, Tango, Telly Monster, Gabrielle, Ji-Young, Tamir, Rosita, Abby Cadabby, Grover and Oscar. Trailer-only friends do not alter the unlocked gameplay roster. A clearly labelled still-photo fallback preserves navigation if WebGL is unavailable. Gameplay uses sculpted, animated 3D puppet-style characters again. Their colours, eyes, noses, costumes and body shapes are informed by the real-character artwork. They are stylised approximations, not production Sesame Workshop models.

## Chapters
- **03 — What do you want?** A new story game based on the supplied book photos. Rosita’s baby brother is crying; help her ask about cookies, bread, rice, ice cream, juice and milk. The first five food questions keep the book’s wording and order; “Do you want milk?” extends the same pattern before the book’s milk ending. Six picture-matching tasks, short walks to randomized food stands, and nine parent-confirmed speaking turns lead to the milk ending. The child then chooses any of the six foods (“I want …”) and finishes with “The baby wants milk.” The first question is “What do you want?” Full models and shorter phrases are optional; no speaking timeout. Planned for about five minutes plus saving/reward time. A separate 3D pretend kitchen/nursery contains a baby puppet, caregiver, low food stands and milk-bottle ending. No uploaded book photos or unrelated personal photos are bundled into the game. Six original vector food pictures support the choices and bank. On completion, the six words join the pending save queue, each requiring the child’s explicit save action. Existing pending words are preserved.
- **01 — Can you find the ball?** Ten-second catch opening, the requested narrator question, ordered searches practising in/on/under, and parent-approved speaking.
- **02 — The A a & B b Garden.** Recognise capital A, small a, capital B and small b separately. Collect a 3D apple, ant, ball and butterfly. World locations and practice order are independently shuffled for every round. The final four-word review order and answer positions are also shuffled. Say the letter form and word on four parent-approved speaking turns. Finally hear and review the four words one at a time. Each picture has four written choices: the correct word, one non-target word with the same starting letter, and two unrelated non-target words. The last activity is vocabulary recognition, not initial-letter sorting. Incorrect taps offer another try, without removing XP. The full targets are “Capital A. Apple.”, “Small a. Ant.”, “Capital B. Ball.” and “Small b. Butterfly.” Shorter-word practice and spoken models are available. About three minutes of planned activity, with no speaking deadline.

After completing the vocabulary game, the child explicitly saves each word on its own memory card, marking it as learnt and remembered. These are child-reported acknowledgements, not an automated assessment. Unsaved cards can be resumed from the vocabulary bank. The bank supports hearing saved words and collecting selected words into named sets for future game creation. It is available from the front page, the desktop game header, and Parent settings.

No character is selected by default. The child must explicitly choose an unlocked friend for each adventure; play is gated until then. All unlocked friends appear in the gallery, and switching during play preserves story progress.

## Controls and parent role
Move with WASD/arrow keys, touch movement keys, or the optional joystick. Approach a marker and use Space/the hand button. Drag the scene to orbit the camera; reset and overview controls remain available. Gameplay dialogue and learning choices sit below the playfield. Rosita returns to a fixed yellow-ring station at the side after the opening catch cinematic; she no longer jumps to each search target. Two small random-colour robot helpers cheer from edge positions. NPC materials are isolated from player materials and fade when a camera-to-player or camera-to-target sightline would be obstructed. No depth-ignoring label sprites are used.

Speaking is manual and parent led. No microphone permission or automatic voice detection is used. Each speaking turn shows Hear pronunciation, Shorter phrase and Parent: accept answer together below the scene. She tries first; the exact target is played only when the model button is tapped. Hearing a full pronunciation model does not lower XP. Shorter-phrase support still contributes to the editable suggested rating. A parent advances the turn directly, with duplicate-tap protection; models never auto-advance. Explicit model playback uses an available American-English TTS voice at a natural pitch and deliberate pace, even if story narration is muted. No child audio or transcripts are recorded, sent or stored. Progress, notes, saved vocabulary, word sets and music preferences live in this browser only.

## Experience and rewards
Parent → Experience & surprise rewards has Add XP (whole numbers 1–5000), Reset XP & level, and Undo last XP change. Reset keeps unlocked friends, notes, vocabulary and earned surprise rewards. Undo restores XP fields only, preserving words and notes saved after the XP change. Each level-up reserves one new-character reward. Three mystery boxes contain hidden candidates from the still-locked roster; only the chosen box unlocks its character. All boxes guarantee a new friend. With fewer than three characters remaining, candidates repeat. Box choices and outstanding rewards persist through reloads. Reveals include animated lids, a light beam, a rising character, confetti and an original short chime; reduced-motion preferences are respected. There are no purchases or losing boxes.

Same-day repeats of the same chapter earn half XP. The first completion of each chapter that day earns full XP. Daily accounting uses Asia/Hong_Kong. A finite roster cannot unlock more characters after all friends are present. Gallery artwork provenance is in `dist/assets/characters/sources.json`.

## Background music
The welcome page, trailer, chapter picker and character selection use a fuller original arrangement: four rotating melodies, bass, chord accompaniment and light synthesized percussion. The front/trailer mix is louder (master target 0.15); gameplay switches to a softer arrangement (0.038). Narration ducks the front mix to 0.032 and gameplay speaking to 0.004. Music has an independent persisted mute preference. Tap Play music once to unlock browser audio, or use Start adventure/trailer controls. Music continues on returning to the front page and suspends while the page is hidden. No external music files are fetched. Pending audio-resume callbacks cannot restart stopped playback.

## Golden sticker shop and wardrobe
Each newly completed adventure awards exactly three Elmo golden stickers when its parent confirms the completion, including same-day replays. The existing duplicate-completion guard prevents double awards. Sticker rewards are independent of XP ratings and XP replay reductions. Existing saved progress migrates with an empty wallet and wardrobe; no notes, words, XP or character unlocks are removed.

The shop offers eight permanent accessories at prices of one to three stickers: bow, sunglasses, flower, explorer hat, star wand, balloon, crown and backpack. Buying debits the wallet once and adds ownership; insufficient funds and repeated purchases cannot debit it. Buying does not automatically equip an item. The wardrobe equips one owned item at a time, supports taking it off without losing ownership, and persists across reloads and character changes. All eight items are rendered as 3D attachments on the player's current character. Carried items follow the right arm (or a trunk-adjacent anchor on Snuffy); wearables have character-adjusted anchors. Trailer NPCs do not wear the player's items. Shop and wardrobe entry points are available on the front page, header, in-game toolbar, parent panel and reward screen. Returning from the shop to the celebration preserves the pending word-saving and mystery-box flow without another award. Wallet, ownership and equipment are browser-local, with no purchases involving money. `node verify-shop.mjs` covers economy, persistence and all eight accessories across the twelve playable models; `node verify.mjs` tests completion/replay awards and duplicate confirmation.

Chapter 03 verification: `node verify-wants.mjs` exercises the complete story through six food questions, distance-checked collections, all nine speaking gates, wrong/duplicate choices, the milk ending, free preference and show-and-tell, single completion reward and all six explicit vocabulary saves. `node verify-scene.mjs` builds and switches all three 3D worlds.

### Refined playhouse family and readable labels
The baby and Mama use dedicated, softly rounded human character models, with fitted eyes, small noses, ears, cheeks, hair, complete hands/feet, a romper/bib and a purple dress. Closed smile/frown curves follow the surface of each head; no flat mouth ovals or mouth squash animation remain. The baby changes from a gentle frown to a closed smile when milk is offered. Labels for all six food stands, the room sign, Baby and Mama use fitted high-resolution text with correct aspect ratio, dark ink on a light background, camera-facing orientation and distance-aware sizing. Labels retain normal depth testing and sit above props rather than over the gameplay UI. `node verify-playhouse-design.mjs` checks mouth clearance, expressions, label fit, contrast and orientation around the camera orbit.


### Body Boogie world missions
Chapter 04 is now a continuous 3D park adventure. Rosita gives missions; walk to the next Sesame Street friend, tap an actual body part on the puppet, and say the corresponding sentence. The parent can enable left/right vocabulary. A relay revises all selected words, followed by a final walk to the dance stage. No separate task window or human diagram appears in gameplay. See DESIGN.md for the standing requirement that future stories use exploration and in-world missions.


### Chapter 05 — open Sound Safari park (Phonics)
Two random parent-enabled A–H letters yield four source words. Round 1 searches by initial sound and accepts either unpractised word in that family; all four words are covered. Round 2 uses two or three NPC-held spelling cards with a coloured initial. The guide plus three friendly original NPCs roam slowly and wait eight seconds before joining a search. They never claim answers. Animals roam, fish swim in a pond, and objects sit on furniture or ground. Fish and fin share one actual 3D model.

Checked phonics clips are optional; parent modelling is explicit when no clip is supplied. Word/phrase models, direct parent approval, categorized notes, rewards and individual word saves remain. No microphone detection or task windows.

`node verify-phonics.mjs` covers all 28 pairs with both card counts, word coverage, NPC pacing, cards, animals, fin interaction, progress and rewards. `node verify-phonics-walk.mjs` follows 196 real-animation-loop routes to moving and static targets. iPad visual/audio playback remains a device verification step.
